When you're working on group projects, we expect you to be working together. This starts with the [planning stages of any project](https://curriculum.codeyourfuture.io/checkpoint/sprints/1/day-plan/#checkpoint-group-day-plan), where you should be engaging directly with your team to break down the project into tasks and allocate work between the members of the team.

You can work on code on your own, but we expect you to be aware of what each other is working on. At your interview, you may be asked about any code in your project, including code someone else wrote.

We **strongly** recommend that you collaborate as you go. Read and discuss each other's code _as you're building_. Do not wait until the end of the project (or the week before the interview). The best way to ensure you maintain that collaborative effort is to use proper branching practices and [review each other's pull requests](https://curriculum.codeyourfuture.io/guides/reviewing/code-review-basics/) for every new feature that is being implemented.

Another way you may choose to collaborate is with [driver/navigator pair programming](/guides/pair-programming/). This is a very common industry practice that can help improve your learning and give you a deeper understanding of the codebase as it is being written. This is not a requirement, but you may want to discuss in your group whether it is something you want and are able to do during the project.
