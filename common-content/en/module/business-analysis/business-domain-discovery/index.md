## Purpose

Practise the early discovery skills a Business Analyst (BA) uses to understand
an unfamiliar business domain before proposing requirements or solutions. By
the end of each interview, the BA should be able to describe:

- the domain, its boundaries, and the problem or opportunity;
- the important business entities and information involved;
- the main process or processes and where difficulties occur;
- the stakeholders involved or affected; and
- the high-level outcomes a future solution should support.

## Set-up

Set a timer for {{<timer>}}5{{</timer>}}.

Work in pairs. Each person chooses a different domain they know from work or personal experience, such as travel, education, a hobby, a club or a regular service. Choose a real, familiar situation and explain it in your own words. No specialist knowledge or preparation is needed. Do not share confidential or sensitive information.

## Discovery interviews

Allow 20 minutes for this activity. Set a timer for {{<timer>}}10{{</timer>}} for each interview.

Interview the first domain expert for 10 minutes. Then switch roles and interview your partner about their different domain for 10 minutes.

### Role 1: Domain expert

You are the person with first-hand knowledge of the chosen domain. Give the BA
an initial overview of what happens and identify a challenge, pain point, or
opportunity worth exploring.

- Explain the business as it works today: who is involved, what they do, and
  what they are trying to achieve.
- Share examples, including relevant exceptions or differences in how things
  happen.
- Answer questions from your experience; it is fine to say when you do not
  know or are unsure.
- Focus on the problem and its context, **not on prescribing a solution**.
  If you find yourself suggesting a tool or feature, explain the underlying
  need instead.

### Role 2: Business Analyst

Lead a curious, systematic interview. Listen, ask open questions, and follow up
on details that affect understanding. Do not jump to requirements or recommend
a solution. You can take brief notes and should check your understanding with
the domain expert before the interview ends.

Use the prompts below as a guide, not a script:

1. **Context and scope:** What is this domain about? Where does the situation
   begin and end? What is in and out of scope?
2. **Problem and goals:** What is difficult or not working well? Who experiences
   the impact? What would a better outcome look like?
3. **People and roles:** Who takes part, makes decisions, provides information,
   or is affected? Are there different needs or perspectives?
4. **Process:** What typically happens, and in what order? What triggers it?
   Where are the hand-offs, delays, rework, or exceptions?
5. **Entities and information:** What important things are managed or changed
   (for example, people, bookings, items, records, or payments)? What
   information is needed, created, or passed between people?
6. **Priorities and constraints:** Which pain point matters most, and why?
   What rules, timing, resources, or external factors shape the situation?

### 10-minute interview plan

| Time | Focus |
|---|---|
| 0–2 minutes | Hear the overview; clarify the domain and problem boundary. |
| 2–4 minutes | Explore goals, pain points, and who is affected. |
| 4–7 minutes | Map the main process, participants, hand-offs, and exceptions. |
| 7–9 minutes | Clarify key entities, information, rules, and constraints. |
| 9–10 minutes | Summarise the problem, stakeholders, process, and desired outcomes; invite corrections. |

After the first 10-minute interview, switch roles and interview your partner. 

## Analyse your notes

Set a timer for {{<timer>}}15{{</timer>}}.

Individually, each BA reviews the notes from their own interview. Summarise the scope, stakeholders, main process, entities, pain points and desired outcomes. Identify gaps and assumptions to check.

## Follow-up and validation

Allow 15 minutes for this activity.

Take turns as the BA and the domain expert, allowing roughly half the time for each domain. Ask follow-up questions, share your summary, invite corrections and record any uncertainty.

Keep the notes from the interview you conducted as the BA for the next workshop.

## Reflection

Allow 5 minutes for this activity.

Briefly compare notes: What question uncovered the most useful detail? What assumption did the BA need to check? Did the interview stay focused on the business problem rather than a proposed solution? Name one question or listening technique to use in a future discovery conversation.
